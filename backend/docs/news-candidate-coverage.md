# Delhi news-reported candidate context

## What is in NAVIRA now

The four source-linked pilot records were removed from the backend fixture and startup seeding. Any news candidates now returned by the API must come from a separate explicit import or live discovery request. The ten most recent complete calendar years are calculated by the API; years without records are reported as “no candidates in loaded data,” never as zero crime. There is no verified ten-year news coverage in this project.

The Delhi Police press archive currently exposes metadata fields for title, district, police station, category, issue date, and a linked document. Its public listing has a filterable date range, but the archive's earliest accessible date and completeness for every year have not been established here. Press releases are selective police communications, not a complete FIR register. NAVIRA does not scrape that archive automatically.

GDELT is connected as the primary live news discovery source through DOC 2.0 ArticleList, with Google News RSS as a fallback if GDELT is rate-limited, unavailable, or has no matching articles. Both search the recent three-month period for crime-related articles indexed with each selected origin/destination locality name. A hit means indexed article text matched a search term; it does **not** establish that the incident happened in that locality. Headline categories are keyword triage only. Results need to be opened, checked against the publisher, and human-reviewed before they can be imported as incident candidates. These indexes are not a census and do not provide ten years of locality-level crime reports. Neither connector requires an API key. If both are unavailable, NAVIRA displays unavailable (or a clearly marked cached snapshot) rather than claiming there are no crimes.

References: [Delhi Police press releases](https://www.delhipolice.gov.in/press-releases.html), [Delhi Police press archive](https://www.delhipolice.gov.in/pressarchive), [GDELT DOC 2.0](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/gdelt-doc-2-0-api-debuts/), [GDELT DOC search updates](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/doc-geo-2-0-api-updates-full-year-searching-and-more/), and [GDELT GEO 2.0](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/gdelt-geo-2-0-api-debuts/).

## API

- `GET /api/v1/news/candidates?locality=CR%20Park&year=2022&limit=100` returns source URL/title/publisher, publication and incident dates, reported category/status, coarse place and review status. Locality filtering is a text match.
- `GET /api/v1/news/coverage` returns the ten-complete-year target, per-year candidate counts, source coverage declarations, and last-check timestamps. The empty-year status means no records in this database, not no incidents.
- `POST /api/v1/news/discover` accepts `{"localities":["CR Park","Welcome"]}` (one or two place names) and returns recent GDELT or Google News RSS article candidates grouped by the exact search term. Results include provider, publisher link, publication time when available, a headline category candidate, `needs_review`, and `unverified_mention_match` location precision. Results with a known publication date outside the recent 93-day window are excluded. Results are cached for 15 minutes; when refresh fails, a cache no older than 24 hours may be returned with `status: stale`. No key is required. The search window is recent three months, not the historical ten-year target.
- `POST /api/v1/routes/locality-news` accepts the selected route's GeoJSON LineString. It reverse-geocodes at most six evenly spaced points (cached and throttled through the Nominatim adapter), deduplicates resolved Delhi locality labels, and searches recent news per locality. Its response includes per-sample resolution status and distance, candidate groups, and a notice that citywide NCRB values are not assigned to localities. Exact neighbourhood boundaries and police-station annual crime counts are not currently loaded.
- `POST /api/v1/routes/locality-reports` remains endpoint-place-name context only. It is not route geometry matching. Existing route risk logic remains unchanged and does not consume these counts.

The Route Planner's **Newspaper incident evidence** panel calls `POST /api/v1/routes/locality-news`. Route samples are checked against Delhi Police station boundary polygons from the official GSDL ArcGIS service, and the resulting station names are compared exactly to the archive's Police Station field. The SCRB New Press Releases listing is paginated; NAVIRA fetches the visible listing page and does not import all 52,000+ rows. Its 10-year date window therefore does not imply complete historical coverage. A no-match result means no candidate was found in the fetched page for that station, not zero registered crimes. Station boundaries are an approximate route match; release metadata does not establish incident coordinates. Items remain `needs_review`, and press releases are selective communications rather than complete FIR counts or crime rates.

The route match to archive items is **text-only**. It does not prove that a route passes through a police station's legal jurisdiction or that an incident occurred on the route. The panel filters listing rows to the last ten complete calendar years, but Delhi Police releases are selective and the current archive listing's historical completeness is unknown; NAVIRA reports the years observed and explicitly avoids claiming full ten-year coverage. Archive reads are cached for 30 minutes, with a stale snapshot allowed for up to seven days and labeled stale. The panel does not count releases as registered crimes and does not calculate locality crime rates. A verified ten-year station dataset and station-jurisdiction boundaries are still needed for that. Sampling does not enumerate every neighborhood. Delhi City NCRB aggregates for 2020–2024 remain separate and are never allocated to route localities. The Community Reports page remains for short-lived user-submitted reports only.

## Import, resume, and coverage metadata

Prepare a locally reviewed JSON file with source metadata and candidate records, then validate it before import:

```powershell
python -m backend.scripts.import_route_safety path\to\reviewed.json --dry-run
python -m backend.scripts.import_route_safety path\to\reviewed.json
```

The importer makes no network requests. Keep stable record IDs and canonical URLs. Rerun the same batch to resume safely: already imported IDs are skipped, new source URLs are attached idempotently, and the per-source checkpoint records the last processed ID. A batch may optionally provide `coverage` with `requested_years`, `processed_years`, `missing_years`, `gap_reasons`, and `note`. These are source-operator declarations and should be based on an actual archive/export check; the API never infers unavailable years from absence of records. Successful imports update `last_checked_at`, record counts, processed years, and checkpoint in `news_source_coverage`.

Record import is separate from the existing text-extraction/classification endpoints. External HTML/PDF must be parsed offline or through an explicitly permitted method, treated as untrusted input, and reduced to minimal metadata and short redacted evidence. Do not store full articles or personal identifiers. GDELT and Google News discovery require no API key; `NAVIRA_GDELT_DOC_URL` can optionally override the server-side GDELT endpoint. Reverse geocoding uses `NAVIRA_GEOCODER_URL` and `NAVIRA_GEOCODER_USER_AGENT`. Do not add provider credentials to frontend environment variables.

## Taxonomy, precision, and interpretation

Keep the source's raw crime wording alongside a cautious normalized label; use `unknown` when a mapping is ambiguous. Current records use statuses such as `reported`, not “verified”; police arrest statements do not establish guilt. Existing review states are `needs_review`, `verified`, and `rejected`; only a human review may change them. Publication date and incident date are separate. Locality and police-station mentions remain context, not precise incident points. Do not assign a locality from a city-only mention, infer coordinates, match a story to a route segment, or use these candidates to rank routes.

NCRB figures in NAVIRA are Delhi City annual registered-case aggregates for 2020–2024. They remain a separate source and geographic level. The project has no verified police-station/neighbourhood annual crime counts for its ten-year target. Neither NCRB city totals nor the number of articles are locality/road crime rates; neither supplies a complete incident denominator or exposure denominator. This feature supports evidence-aware locality context only, not a verified locality-level crime-rate claim.
